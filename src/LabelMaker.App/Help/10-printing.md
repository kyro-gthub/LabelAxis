# Printing

`Ctrl+P` (or `Print` at the top right) opens the Print window. The picture on the right shows the first sheet exactly as it will print; `Preview…` shows every sheet.

## What to print

- **Fill the sheet with the master label:** the same label in every spot.
- **One label per data record:** a different label for each row of your data (see [[data|Data]]).
- **Pages:** several different pages on one sheet, one after another:
  - **All pages**, **Current page** (the page you were looking at), or **Selected pages** (tick them in the list).
  - Each page has its own **copies** box: page 1 can print 6 times and page 2 twelve times on the same sheets. **Copies of every page** sets them all at once. The numbers are saved with the project.

> Tip: Pressing Print while a page (not the master label) is on screen opens the Print window set to that page already.

## Start position and ranges

`Start position and page range…` (also `Print > Advanced Print Settings…`) starts on a later spot of a sheet that is partly used, skips spots, and chooses which records or pages to print. **Sheets … to …** reprints just some sheets, for example one that jammed.

## Paper and margins

Normally labels go where the sheet template says. Choose another **paper size**, **landscape**, or **use my own margins and gaps** when your sheet differs from the template.

## Copies, scale and outlines

- **Copies** prints the whole job more than once.
- **Scale** stays at 100% for labels to come out the right size; change it only to correct a printer, better with [[calibration|calibration]].
- In `Advanced…`, **label outlines** and **crop marks** draw the edges of each label, handy on plain paper.

## Printers

The list holds the printers installed in Windows and any [[wifi-printers|Wi-Fi printers]] you added. `Windows dialog…` opens the printer's own settings (duplex, tray). Thermal label printers have their own window; see [[thermal|Thermal label printers]].

## History

`History…` (or `Print > Print History…`) lists earlier jobs; reprint one, or finish a job that stopped part-way.
