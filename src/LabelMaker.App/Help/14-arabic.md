# Arabic text and fonts

## Writing Arabic

Type Arabic in any text box. A paragraph that starts with an Arabic (or Hebrew) letter runs right to left by itself:

- the letters join as they should;
- words read from the right, and wrapped lines continue from the right;
- numbers, and English words such as a model name, keep their own order inside the Arabic.

To make a whole text box right to left whatever it starts with, set `Text Settings > Direction` to **RightToLeft**.

> Tip: Alignment is not changed for you: choose **Right** under `Align` for Arabic text that should sit against the right edge.

Arabic letters are never spread apart by **letter spacing**, because that would break the joins.

## Arabic fonts that come with LabelAxis

These five are built into LabelAxis, so labels look the same on every PC whether or not Windows has them:

| Font | Style |
|---|---|
| Tajawal | Clean and modern, light to read |
| IBM Plex Sans Arabic | Modern, business-like |
| Almarai | Strong; good for prices and headings |
| Amiri | Classic Naskh, formal |
| Scheherazade New | Traditional Naskh, very readable |

They appear in every font list next to the fonts installed in Windows (Windows itself often has Tahoma, Arial, Segoe UI, Dubai, Traditional Arabic and others).

## Printing and PDF

Arabic prints and exports exactly as it looks on screen, on sheets, thermal printers and in PDF files.
